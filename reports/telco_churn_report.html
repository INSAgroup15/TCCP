Telco Customer Churn — Cleaning, Feature Engineering & a Five-Model SHAP Benchmark

Source data: the public Kaggle "Telco Customer Churn" dataset (IBM sample, blastchar/telco-customer-churn, 7,043 customers × 21 raw columns). This report cleans it, engineers it up to 32 model-ready features, and benchmarks five tabular classifiers with SHAP explainability, following the design of Zerine et al. (2026), "Explainable churn prediction in telecom with tabular ML five model benchmark and SHAP analysis," Discover Artificial Intelligence 6, 263.

rows: 7,043  ·  raw columns: 21  ·  final feature columns: 32  ·  target: Churn (26.5% positive)

01Dataset & cleaning

The raw file has 7,043 customer rows and 21 columns: a customer identifier, demographics, account/contract fields, up to eight service subscriptions, and billing amounts, with Churn (Yes/No) as the target.

  1. Blank TotalCharges. 11 rows had a blank string instead of a number — all 11 have tenure == 0 (brand-new customers who haven't been billed yet). Trimmed and cast to 0.0 rather than dropped, so no rows are lost.
  2. Identifier removed. customerID is dropped — a unique ID carries no generalizable signal and is a textbook leakage risk if it were ever correlated with row order or a join key.
  3. Redundant categories collapsed. Seven add-on columns (OnlineSecurity, TechSupport, etc.) encode "No internet/phone service" as a separate category, but that's fully determined by InternetService/PhoneService already being "No" — so those levels are collapsed into "No" and the fields become clean binary flags.
  4. Binary Yes/No → 1/0 for all remaining flag fields, including the target.
  5. Ordinal Contract encoding (month-to-month=0 < one year=1 < two year=2), since contract length has a genuine order that a plain one-hot would throw away.
  6. One-hot encoding (reference level dropped) for the remaining nominal fields: gender, InternetService, PaymentMethod.
A row-level duplicate check (excluding customerID) finds 22 rows that share identical feature values with another row under a different ID — expected in a 7k-row categorical-heavy table, not a data-quality problem, since IDs themselves are unique.

Those ticket-derived features are called out in the paper as second only to tenure and contract in SHAP importance, so their absence here is the main reason the benchmark below lands below the paper's reported number (see §4).

03Five-model benchmark

Same model roster as the paper — Logistic Regression (Elastic Net), SVC (RBF), Random Forest, XGBoost, and a Multilayer Perceptron — each tuned with randomized search (PR-AUC objective, stratified inner CV) on an 80/20 train/test split, with the decision threshold chosen on training out-of-fold predictions to maximize F1, exactly as in the paper's protocol, then applied unchanged to the held-out test set.

Test set: 1,409 held-out customers (26.5% churn). Threshold chosen to maximize F1 on training data, which favors recall — this is why accuracy here reads lower than a plain 0.5-cutoff would give (see box below).

At a standard 0.5 probability cutoff instead of the F1-optimal threshold, accuracy moves to: SVC 80.2%, MLP 79.4%, Random Forest 76.7%, XGBoost 74.7%, Logistic Regression 74.0% — precision rises and recall falls for every model. This 79–80% range matches what most published benchmarks on this exact public dataset report.

Comparing to the paper

The paper's multilayer perceptron reports 92.28% accuracy (precision 0.88, recall 0.82, F1 0.85) under full nested cross-validation on their 34-feature / 8,093-row expanded dataset. This reproduction's best model, Neural Network (MLP), reaches 78.1% accuracy on the public 32-feature dataset. That ~14-point gap is consistent with the paper's own related-work summary, which places typical public-dataset benchmarks (no ticket data) in the "low-to-high eighties," and attributes their own jump to 92% mainly to the technical-ticket signal and the larger, richer sample — not to the five model families themselves, which is why the SHAP rankings below still line up closely with theirs.
